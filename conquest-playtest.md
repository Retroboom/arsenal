# Conquest (playtest v3, 15 Jun 2026)

The default pick-up scenario for HoF. Bring whatever you've got; a two-minute setup turns two unequal forces into a fair-feeling fight, no points, no arguing over who's stronger. Normal HoF rules and turn sequence; only the setup, objectives, and break points change.

**Quick terms**
- Unit = platoon.
- BL (Break Limit) = 5 + 2 per platoon. Run out of BL in break points and you rout, you lose.
- BP roll = 1d6: 1-3 = 1, 4-5 = 2, 6 = 3.
- The Clock = the turn by which the Attacker must rout the Defender, or the Attacker loses.

## Setup (in order)

1. **Terrain.** Alternate placing pieces until you both agree it's enough; cover in both halves. You don't know your side yet, so play it straight.
2. **Roles + Clock.** Agree who's the underdog (the weaker force for this fight, you'll usually know). Can't agree? Fewer platoons is the underdog; still tied, roll off. **The underdog picks Attack or Defend**; the other takes the opposite. Then set the **Clock** (default 8; try 6-10). *(For a badly lopsided pairing, run the optional Quick Parity at the bottom instead of eyeballing it.)*
3. **Attacker drops objectives.** Count = half the Defender's platoons (round up) + 2. Each sits on or touching a real feature: village, woods, hill, crossroads, building, a field. Spread them: 12"+ apart, 12"+ off any edge. You place before you know your edge, so you can't farm a corner.
4. **Defender picks an edge.** Attacker takes the opposite. Defender controls every objective to start.
5. **Reserves.** Defender holds half their platoons (round down) off-table, vehicles first. From turn 3 they roll them on per normal reserves.
6. **Deploy and play.**

## Objectives

- **Capture:** any Team within 4", with no enemy Team within 4", takes control. Vehicles capture too.
- **Take one off the enemy → they roll 2 BP.** Both ways: you capping theirs, them re-capping yours.
- Grabbing a neutral (unheld) objective: nothing.
- *Variant ("infantry takes ground"): only infantry and gun Teams capture; vehicles contest and hold (deny within 4") but never score. Favours infantry-heavy forces, use it for a Normandy feel, not as the default.*

## Break Points (only when something happens)

- Your unit drops below half → you roll 1.
- Your unit dies → you roll 1 (2 if it got wiped from full strength in one go).
- Enemy takes an objective off you → you roll 2.
- No end-of-turn rolls.

## Winning

- A force routs the instant its break points pass its BL. That force loses.
- **Defender routs first → Attacker wins.**
- **Attacker routs first → Defender wins.**
- **Clock runs out with nobody routed → Defender wins.** The Attacker has to break the enemy in time; the Defender just has to live to the buzzer.

## Why it's built this way (so we don't relitigate at the table)

- **Role is the handicap.** The underdog picks the seat they'd rather sit in; the stronger force takes what's left. That one choice does more balancing than any Break Limit transfer, and because the underdog chooses, it self-corrects to whichever role turns out to be the comfortable one.
- **The Clock is the pace-setter.** A force that takes the attack has to close the deal; a turtle on defence only has to survive. No dawdling, clean end.
- **Objectives are how you break the enemy fast.** Each capture is two break-point rolls on the loser, so taking ground is a real road to a rout, right alongside just killing things. Vehicles can capture, so an armour force has a path in the objective game too (that's the default; the infantry-only variant is the exception, not the rule).
- **Defender reserves half (vehicles first),** so the opening turns are the Attacker's window and the Defender's heavy stuff counterpunches from turn 3.

## Dials (tune by feel across games)

- **The Clock** (the main one): shorter favours the Defender, longer favours the Attacker.
- **Objective count** (the +2): more = more for the Attacker to work with. Also the lever for how much the game rewards capturing versus killing. Turn it down if infantry-spam starts winning outright.
- **Reserve timing** (turn 3): earlier = stronger Defender comeback and more Attacker pressure.
- **By-feel BL bump:** if one side keeps winning no matter which role they pick, give the other +2 BL and move on. No formula. Trust the table.

## Optional: Quick Parity (for lopsided pairings)

When the forces look badly mismatched and you'd rather size the gap than eyeball it, this takes about thirty seconds and replaces the "agree who's the underdog" step.

1. Lay both forces facing each other.
2. Pair off top-down by eye: biggest threat vs biggest threat. No precise ranking needed; only the lopsided pairs matter.
3. Score a pair only when one unit is clearly the scarier to face (out-ranges and out-penetrates it, far tougher, or much bigger). If you have to deliberate, it's even, score nothing.
4. **+2 per extra (unpaired) unit** the larger force brings.
5. Net the totals. **The lower total is the underdog** and picks the role. For each point the higher total still leads by, give the underdog +2 BL or tighten the Attacker's Clock by 1; a lead of 1 is usually covered by the role pick alone.

Why +2 per unit and why count bodies: Conquest is won by breaking the enemy, and capturing helps do it, so numbers matter more than raw quality. A generic "who has the better army" read would point the wrong way.

**Worked example** (Germany: Tiger x2, Panther x3, PzIV x3, Grenadiers, PaK40, 5 platoons. Soviet: Rifle x10, Rifle x8, SMG, T-34/85 x3, 76mm x3, 45mm x3, 6 platoons):

| Pair | scarier | score |
|---|---|---|
| Tiger / T-34/85 | Tiger | G +1 |
| Panther / 76mm | Panther | G +1 |
| PzIV / 45mm | PzIV | G +1 |
| PaK40 / Rifle (10) | the big platoon | S +1 |
| Grenadiers / Rifle (8) | Soviet bigger | S +1 |
| extra: SMG | unpaired, +2 | S +2 |

Germany 3, Soviet 4 → **Soviet +1, so Germany is the underdog and picks its role.** Lead of 1 is covered by the pick; no BL nudge. Set the Clock and play.
