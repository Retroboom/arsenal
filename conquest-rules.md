# Conquest (playtest draft, 13 Jun 2026)

Pick-up scenario for HoF. Bring whatever you've got, get a fair fight in a couple minutes. No points, no arguing over who's stronger, no playing each other's models. Normal HoF turn sequence; only the setup and the break points change.

**Quick terms**
- Unit = platoon.
- BL (Break Limit) = 5 + 2 per platoon. Hit your BL in break points and you rout, you lose.
- BP roll = 1d6: 1-3 scores 1, 4-5 scores 2, 6 scores 3.

## Setup (in order)

1. **Terrain.** Cover on both halves.
2. **Bid for the attack.** Start at 0, take turns bumping +1 or passing. Your bid = the BL you'll give up to be the Attacker. High bid wins it: you're the Attacker, drop your BL by your bid. Other guy's the Defender. Both pass at 0 = whoever's got more vehicles attacks, no cost.
   - Why fight for it? Only the Attacker scores off objectives, and scoring is how you break the other guy. A fast force with infantry to hold ground wants the attack; a slow gunline is happy to sit.
3. **Attacker drops the objectives.** Count = half the Defender's platoons (round down) + 2. Spread them: 12"+ apart, 12"+ off any edge. You're placing before you know which edge is yours, so you can't stack your own corner.
4. **Defender picks an edge.** Attacker takes the opposite. Defender controls every objective to start.
5. **Reserves.** Defender holds half their platoons (round down) off-table, vehicles first. From turn 3 they roll to bring one on per turn (normal reserves rules).
6. **Deploy, play till someone routs.**

## Objectives

- Grab one: infantry or gun team within 4", no enemy team within 4". Vehicles can park on it to deny (contest), but can't grab or hold.
- Take one off the enemy and they roll **2 BP**. Both ways: you capping theirs, them re-capping yours.
- Grabbing a neutral one (nobody holds it) = nothing. Flip them as often as you like.

## Break Points (only roll when something happens)

- Your unit drops below half → you roll 1.
- Your unit dies → you roll 1 (2 if it got wiped outright from full).
- Enemy takes an objective off you → you roll 2.
- No end-of-turn rolls.

Pass your BL = rout = lose.
*(Want a hard ending? Cap at 6 turns. If nobody's routed, lower BP-to-BL ratio wins; tie goes to most objectives held.)*

## Why it hangs together

- Attacker has to walk out into the open to take objectives, right where the Defender's guns are waiting. That exposure is the price of scoring.
- Defender bleeds when shoved off ground, but can claw it back by re-capping, which also punishes an Attacker who grabs stuff he can't hold.
- More platoons = more BL, but if you're defending, more objectives to lose too. Quality that holds its ground keeps that BL; a swarm that folds just hands it back.
- The bid is the handicap, set by you two, not a formula. The weaker side's threat to take the attack makes the stronger side pay for it.
- No attacker clock. The pressure is the Defender's reserves piling on from turn 3, so dawdling costs you.

## What to watch (testing)

- **Win rate by role** should land roughly even. Defender winning a lot → bump the "+2" objectives or the capture rolls. Attacker running away → drop them.
- **Stalls.** Attacker grabs the easy objectives then sits and the game drags → reserves weren't enough. Back-pocket fix: Attacker rolls 1 BP at the end of each turn he doesn't hold every objective.
- **Counterpunch.** Reserves showing up too late or too rarely to take anything back → start reserve rolls on turn 2, or ease the check.
- **Pure mismatch.** One side literally can't hurt the other → that's a list problem (bring AT), not a scenario one.
- **Flippy objectives.** One hot objective flipping back and forth can dump a pile of BP both ways. If one chokepoint runs the game, spread placement wider or drop to 1 roll per cap.

## Dials

- **Objective surplus** (the +2): more = more attacker help.
- **Rolls per cap** (2): how hard losing ground hurts. Lose your whole line and you're nearly broken, on purpose. Drop to 1 if objectives feel too swingy.
- **Reserve timing** (turn 3): earlier = more attacker pressure + a stronger defender comeback.

## Optional: Weight count (so the strongest stuff isn't free)

Points-free way to make monsters cost something. Fast, because only standouts count, everything normal scores 0. It's comparative, so matched power cancels.

Each platoon scores 1 Weight for **each** thing it's got. A Tiger = heavy armor + big gun = 2:
- vehicle with **front+flank armor 6+** (Tiger, Panther, IS-2, King Tiger)
- team with **AT 6+** (the biggest guns + tank hunters)
- **Elite**
- **infantry platoon of 10+ teams** (also stops you cramming bodies into one fat cheap platoon)

Extra flags if you want more reach: **FP 3** (flame, heavy howitzers), **autocannon / quad AA**, **medium/heavy or off-board artillery**, **sniper**.

Total each side's Weight. Heavier force drops BL by **1 per point of difference**. Two Tiger forces both run heavy and cancel; a Tiger force vs a Sherman force pays the gap.

Why it works: the bars sit just over "really good but not a monster," so StuG, Sherman 76, PaK40, and PzIV all score 0 and make a free backbone, while the monsters cost you unless the enemy matched them. It's the FPP's your-best-vs-mine comparison, just counted instead of argued.

Dials: BL per point (1 to start); raise the bars to only tax super-heavies; per-platoon (a Tiger platoon = 2 whatever its size, simplest) vs per-team (3 Tigers = 6, folds numbers in too).
