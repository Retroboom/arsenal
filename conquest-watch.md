# Conquest: what to watch (playtest sheet)

Print it, jot a few lines after each game. The sim took us as far as math can; the rest is table-only.

## Log each game

- Forces, rough: who had more platoons / more armour / more infantry.
- Clock named (turn #), and who chose to Attack.
- Winner, and how: Defender routed (Attacker win), Attacker routed (Defender win), or Clock ran out (Defender win).
- Turn the game ended.

## The questions the sim couldn't answer

1. **Win rate by role.** Over a handful of games, do Attackers and Defenders win about evenly? If one role keeps winning, the Clock is set wrong: shorten it if Attackers dominate, lengthen it if Defenders do.
2. **Was the crossing survivable?** The big unknown. Can attacking infantry actually walk open ground into tank and MG fire and arrive in shape to capture? If the attack just dies in the open, the scenario favors the Defender harder than any model said. Note how many attacking platoons were still combat-effective when they reached the objectives.
3. **Did the Attacker stall?** Grab the easy objectives then sit = the Clock isn't biting. Routing the Defender with turns to spare = Clock too long. Hitting the Clock with no rout but a real fight = Clock about right.
4. **Did letting vehicles capture work?** Did armour taking and holding ground make armour-heavy lists fun and viable, without making infantry feel pointless? If infantry stop mattering, try the infantry-only variant.
5. **Hidden setup / ambush.** Did Defenders sitting concealed and firing first wreck the attack? (The sim ignored this completely; suspected to favor the Defender a lot.)
6. **Smoke and artillery.** Did the Attacker smoke the crossing? Did indirect fire swing things? (Also unmodeled; suspected to be the Attacker's main counter to #2.)
7. **Spreading a platoon.** Did anyone fan a platoon across objectives? Did it grab more ground or just get picked apart?

## Red flags → quick fixes

- Defender wins most games → shorten the Clock 1-2, or start reserves on turn 2.
- Attacker wins most games → lengthen the Clock, or drop one objective.
- Games feel decided by turn 3 → BLs too low for your group's lethality; bump everyone +2.
- Games drag with no rout → that's the Clock's job; if it still drags, shorten it.
- One force wins whatever role it takes → by-feel +2 BL to the other side, no formula.

## Open design questions we're actually testing

- Is "underdog picks the role" enough to balance on its own, or do lopsided pairings still need the Quick Parity nudge?
- Does "Attacker places objectives, Defender picks edge" reliably produce meaningful, central objectives, or do people still find a way to game it?
- Does the scenario reward a spread of compositions, or does one kind of list (infantry-spam, all-armour) quietly dominate? If so, the objective count is the dial.
- Is the default pregame setup actually 2-5 minutes at the table, or does anything in it bog down?
