# Terrain Generator (playtest draft, 14 Jun 2026)

Procedural board setup for a 6' x 4' table (72" x 48"). Roll it in order. Goal: believable Western Europe / Normandy boards in a couple minutes, no fussing.

## Build order
Roads, then fields, then buildings, then bocage, then woods, then objectives. Each step respects what's already down.

## Density
Fill the table. These rules can produce sparse boards, so aim fuller. Target roughly a quarter to a third of the table under terrain. Practical guide for a 6x4: 2-3 fields, 2 building clusters, 60" to 90"+ of bocage, and several woods, arranged so no open stretch runs wider than about 12" to 18" (a unit should always have cover within a move). Lean toward more, then thin out only if a board plays like a maze.

## Roads (3" thick)
- Always lay one road that runs from one table edge to the other.
- 15% chance that road has a single curve, a bend of 15deg to 45deg.
- 50% chance the road has an intersection somewhere along it.
  - If it does: 50% it's a T, otherwise a 4-way.
- From each intersection, branch a new road that follows the same rules (curve check, intersection check).
- Space successive intersections at irregular intervals of 24" to 48" of road.
- A branch runs to a table edge or ties into another road. It doesn't dead-end in open ground (unless you want a farm track, optional).
- Stop branching when the remaining run is under 24". On a 6x4 this lands at roughly 1-2 intersections.

## Fields
- Place 1 to 3 fields.
- Each is an irregular four-sided shape (not a tidy rectangle).
- Open/crop ground.
- Randomize each field's size independently: scale it between 50% and 150% of the baseline, so no two boards have the same field footprint. If a scaled field would cross a table edge, road, or another piece (minus the 1" gap), slide it inward or trim the scale until it fits.

## Low crops
- A second field type: same irregular four-sided shape, but depicting planted rows.
- Render as alternating brown (soil) and green (crop) stripes.
- Treat exactly like a field for spacing, hedges, size randomization, and objectives.

## Buildings
- Place a hamlet and/or a farmstead.
- Site them near a road. They double as objective anchors.

## Bocage (2" thick)
- Total length across all hedgerows is at least 36" (on a full board, 60" to 90"+, per Density).
- Each hedge: 75% chance it starts from a table edge.
- Each hedge: 75% chance it borders a road or a field (runs alongside) rather than standing free.
- A hedge that borders a field is effectively that field's boundary, which is why fields are placed first.
- Snap to terrain edges. If a hedge would run alongside the straight edge of a field or road but at a slightly different angle, measure the angle between them. Within 45deg: rotate the hedge so its angle matches the edge exactly (truly parallel, not just close) and offset it so the gap is exactly 1". More than 45deg off: the hedge is crossing the feature, not running along it, so leave it or break it at the crossing.
- No silly-short stubs; keep each run a sensible length.

## Woods
- Irregular round or oblong shapes.
- 5" up to 12" long, up to 6" wide. (Read "5 across," not 5" circumference.)
- Smaller woods imply dense terrain: the smaller they are, the more they want to clump. Small copses come in batches of at least 4, grouped together (reads as one thick wood broken by gaps).
- Each small dense wood sits exactly 1" from its neighbours in the clump (no more, no less), and each must touch that distance to at least 2 other woods. This forces tight thickets rather than scattered dots.
- 50% chance any given wood sits within 5" of another wood.

## Objectives (Conquest)
- Count = half the Defender's platoons (round up) + 2.
- At least 12" apart and at least 12" from every table edge.
- Anchor them to features (junctions, buildings, hill, field, woods) where the spacing allows. A feature outside the 12" margin simply gets no objective.

## Legality / common sense
- Keep a minimum 1" gap between any two terrain pieces, so nothing overlaps or touches. The two controlled exceptions sit at exactly 1": a hedge bordering its field or road (see Bocage), and dense copses within a clump (see Woods).
- Terrain sits alongside roads, not on top of them. Buildings and bocage leave a clear gap from the carriageway (roughly the road's own width). The only things that cross a road are a deliberate hedge gap or a road running into a village.
- Woods don't sit on roads.
- Bocage crosses a road only at a gap (a real break in the hedge).
- Objectives always obey the 12"/12" rule even if it pulls them off a feature.
